# System Analysis — Generation 2

Generation 2 achieved a new best of C=1.5091 (explore_1/sol03), breaking through the 1.5108 barrier via the coarse-to-fine + warm smooth-max combination. The pipeline's coverage matrix correctly identified this as the highest-priority unexplored combination, and the strategy paid off. However, gen2 also surfaces three recurring systemic problems that, if unaddressed, will cost compute in gen3 and beyond.

## Missing Capabilities

### [CRITICAL] No runtime estimation before committing to compute budget

**Evidence:** full_1 self-reported: "A way to estimate runtime before committing to a heavy pipeline." explore_1 self-reported: "Exact time budget per solution: I had to estimate 600s limit, but the exact per-seed runtime wasn't predictable without profiling." explore_2 noted similar budget uncertainty.

**Impact:** Without knowing ~steps/sec (which varies ~2-4× depending on JAX/hardware), agents cannot reliably estimate if a proposed pipeline will complete within the timeout. This caused the full_1 failure and explore_1/sol03 timeout.

**Fix needed:** A lightweight profiling script (e.g., `python3 problem/profile.py`) that runs evaluate.py on a tiny dummy solution and reports steps/second. Alternatively, add a note to the brief with the observed step rate from previous agents.

---

### [MODERATE] No function visualization helper

**Evidence:** gen1_agent_gaps #3, gen2_agent_gaps #4. Three gen2 agents noted inability to visualize optimized functions. This has been flagged for two consecutive generations with no action.

**Impact:** Agents cannot reason about what the 1.5091 solution's function looks like, making initialization design for gen3 guesswork rather than informed design.

---

### [MODERATE] SA acceptance rate not logged

**Evidence:** gen2_agent_gaps #3. explore_2 was "tuning SA hyperparameters blind."

**Impact:** SA experiments cannot be properly diagnosed or improved without knowing whether proposals are being accepted (too cold) or rejected (too hot).

## Prompt Problems

### [CRITICAL] full.md does not enforce "cheapest first" rule

**Evidence:** Two consecutive full_1 agents produced over-engineered solutions that timed out. Both agents self-identified the same fix: "Start with a lighter sol01 to get a scored result quickly, then add complexity in sol02/sol03."

**Fix:** full.md must include an explicit constraint: "Your first solution (sol01.py) MUST be a minimal, fast-running variant that completes evaluation in < 60 seconds. Use it to establish a baseline. Only in sol02+ should you add complexity."

---

### [MINOR] Cold vs warm fine-stage knowledge gap persisted across gen1→gen2

**Evidence:** explore_1/sol01 started the fine stage cold (T=0.001) and scored 1.5188 — no improvement. This should have been predictable from the gen1 dead-ends list ("Multi-scale with gradient descent at coarse stage: 1.5270-1.5730"). The principle — don't use cold optimization after upsampling — wasn't explicitly stated.

**Fix:** Add to State of Affairs or known dead-ends: "After upsampling from coarse to fine, the fine stage MUST restart with warm smooth-max temperature (T≥0.01). Cold fine stage nullifies the coarse initialization benefit."

### [MODERATE] explore_2's SA investigation shows poor return-on-investment

explore_2's best result was C=1.5108 — matching gen1 best, zero improvement. Three solutions, all requiring 4-8 seed initializations + SA overhead, for no net gain. The approach was reasonable to test, but the gen1 agent_gaps (#5) already identified SA as the highest-priority gap and recommended "coarse-scale SA (N=23)" — the literature approach. explore_2 implemented fine-grid SA instead, which was predictably ineffective given the known sticky-basin problem at N=600.

**Root cause:** The brief or agent misread the literature recommendation. Boyer et al. do SA at N=23, not N=600. The brief should have specified this constraint explicitly.

---

### [MINOR] Hard diminishing returns on restart count confirmed

exploit_1 confirmed: 16 restarts → 1.5107 (vs 8 restarts → 1.5108), 20 restarts → 1.5108. Increasing restarts beyond 8 is not cost-effective with Gaussian-bump initializations. This is now strongly established and should be reflected in the knowledge base (pattern or idea update) to prevent gen3 agents from repeating the experiment.

## Experiment Gaps

### [HIGH PRIORITY — NOT YET TRIED] Coarse-scale SA (N=30-80) → upsample → smooth-max fine

All three productive gen2 agents independently recommended this as their highest priority experiment for gen3. The evaluator also lists it as #1. This is Boyer et al.'s actual algorithm. No agent has tried it.

**Expected information gain:** Either (a) finds basins below 1.5091 by exploring the low-dimensional coarse landscape with SA before upsampling, or (b) confirms that even the coarse landscape has the same sticky-basin problem, narrowing the search.

---

### [HIGH PRIORITY — NOT YET TRIED] Warm-start polish from 1.5091 solution

Load gen002_explore_1_sol03's output array, continue with tighter temperature schedule (T=0.0003→0.0001→0.00003, 50k steps each). Fast (uses cached solution), safe, may push from 1.5091 toward 1.505.

**Expected information gain:** Tests whether 1.5091 is a strict local minimum or whether extended low-temperature annealing can continue improving.

---

### [MEDIUM PRIORITY — NOT YET TRIED] Non-Gaussian coarse initialization shapes

All gen2 explore agents used Gaussian-bump or random-noise initialization. The gen1 agent_gaps identified "arcsine-shaped functions" from literature. Comb functions, step functions, half-domain support, and random Fourier modes have not been tried at the coarse stage.

**Expected information gain:** Different initialization families may find structurally different coarse basins that survive upsampling to N=600.

---

### [MEDIUM PRIORITY — RETRIEVAL NEEDED] AlphaEvolve warm-start array

research_1 (gen1) identified that AlphaEvolve published their 600-interval array achieving C=1.5032. A warm-start from a known-good external solution is the fastest path to the target. This was listed in gen1 agent_gaps #6 and never actioned.

**Expected information gain:** High — either the array is retrievable (instant progress) or not (rules out this approach). Low-cost experiment.

---

### [MEDIUM PRIORITY — UNTESTED] Fourier-basis parameterization + smooth-max

explore_2 gen1 used Fourier basis without smooth-max (1.5294). The coverage matrix shows "Fourier + smooth-max" as untested. A 20-parameter Fourier representation may explore the landscape more efficiently at coarse scale.
