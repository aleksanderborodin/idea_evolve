# Observations — Track B MITM Exploration

## Approaches Tried

### 1. Naive MITM BFS (sol01, sol02)
- Ran bidirectional BFS from initial state and solved state
- Tried various depth limits (5, 6, 7, 8) and beam sizes
- **Result**: Did not find paths shorter than compressed sample. MITM search was too slow for depth > 6 due to branching factor of 24.

### 2. Beam Search (sol03, sol04, sol05)
- Used `cayleypy_beam_solver` with various parameters (beam_width 1000-4000, max_steps 150-300)
- Multi-restart approaches
- **Result**: Beam search did not find shorter paths than compressed sample. Either beam returned None or returned longer paths.

### 3. Move Cancellation Compression (sol01 final)
- The key insight: sample_submission paths are exact inverses of random walks
- Random walks contain internal cancellations (X.-X patterns)
- Iteratively removing adjacent inverse pairs shortens paths for free
- **Result**: Best approach - 46312 vs 50572 baseline (8.4% improvement)

## Key Findings

1. **Move cancellation is the primary win**: The sample_submission paths contain significant cancellation patterns. Removing adjacent X.-X pairs iteratively gives 8.4% improvement without any search.

2. **Beam search doesn't beat compression**: The cayleypy beam solver with various parameters (width 1000-4000, steps 150-300) didn't find paths shorter than compressed sample for any puzzle.

3. **MITM BFS is intractable for depth > 10**: With branching factor 24, even depth 6 on each side (combined 12) is millions of states. Pruning is essential but loses optimality guarantees.

4. **Sample paths are optimal inverses**: Since sample_submission is the exact inverse of the scramble walk, it's always a valid path. But random walks have cancellations, so the inverse also has cancellations.

## Why Compression Works

The Kaggle test was generated by random walks from solved state. The sample_submission is the inverse of that walk. Random walks of length N have expected ~N/3 cancellations (every 3rd move cancels with its inverse). So the inverse also has ~N/3 cancellations, which we remove.

## What Didn't Work

- Deep MITM (depth > 6) - memory/explosion
- Beam search with beam_width < 5000 - too narrow
- Multi-restart beam - still didn't beat compression
- Various hybrid approaches
