## Key Findings

1. **idea_013 (combined recipe) tested and found marginal:** Score 44111 — worse than
   gen003's best of 44094. The recipe works mechanically but training data depth is
   the binding constraint.

2. **Training data depth is the core bottleneck:** A predictor trained on depth ≤50
   data (BFS or random walks) has zero guidance power for very_hard puzzles (depth
   501–1000, 74.8% of score). Both BFS-only and RW-depth-50 predictors confirmed.

3. **idea_010 corrected:** BFS depth-6 data is NOT strictly superior to random walks
   for predictor training. It's useful for MITM but useless as sole training source.

4. **CayleyPy's MlpModel uses one-hot, not raw integers:** Two generations of our
   belief that "the library model uses raw integers" was wrong. The fix is to use the
   library's proven model (idea_014), not a custom embedding approach (idea_011).

5. **Beam width is the dominant parameter:** The CayleyPy RL paper is explicit: solution
   quality scales almost linearly with log(beam_width). We used beam_width=4096.
   Competitive solutions likely use 65536+.

6. **Non-backtracking quadruples success rate:** Advanced beam mode with non-backtracking
   increases success rate from 17.6% to 69.7% in the paper. Mutually exclusive with MITM.
