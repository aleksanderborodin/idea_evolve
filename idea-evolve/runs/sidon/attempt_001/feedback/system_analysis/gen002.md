# System Analysis — Generation 2

## Executive Summary

Generation 2 was the most successful generation to date: +3 improvement (99→102), 100% valid
solutions (vs 18% invalid in gen 1), helper infrastructure deployed, and Singer constructions
fully exhausted. The pipeline functioned well at a strategic level. The issues identified below
are coordination and knowledge-flow problems that impede efficiency, not fundamental failures.

---

## Pipeline Problems

### [MODERATE] Duplicate Singer q=101 implementation

**Observation:** exploit_1 and exploit_2 both independently implemented the full GF(101³) Singer
construction from scratch. Both achieved 102. exploit_2's sol01 (SA from 99-element seed) was
architecturally planned as a complement, but both agents ended up implementing the same q=101
machinery in parallel.

**Evidence:** exploit_1 report: "Implemented GF(101³)... Polynomial x³-3x-1". exploit_2 report:
"Implemented the GF(101³) Singer difference set construction." Both reimplemented ~40 lines of
GF arithmetic that the experimentator was simultaneously building as a shared helper.

**Root cause:** The architect acknowledged this risk ("SA from 99-seed might be premature") but
accepted it since agents couldn't share work at runtime. The exploit_2 brief likely didn't
strongly constrain it to SA-only — it appears exploit_2 pivoted to Singer q=101 after SA failed.

**Severity:** Moderate. The wasted work produced valid results, but ~100 lines of GF arithmetic
were written 3× in one generation (exploit_1, exploit_2, experimentator_1).

---

### [MODERATE] Brief contained known-dead-end formulas for Ruzsa and Bose-Chowla

**Observation:** explore_1's brief presented Ruzsa ({a·p + a²%p}) and Bose-Chowla ({i·p + g^i%p})
as valid constructions to implement. The state_of_affairs already contained a dead-end note:
"Parabola/quadratic-residue constructions: Mathematically incorrect for large primes." The
Architect failed to translate this dead-end into a brief constraint.

**Evidence:** explore_1 report: "The brief's Ruzsa and Bose-Chowla formulas are wrong... The
dead-end note in state_of_affairs is correct and covers both." agent_gaps gen002: "explore_1's
brief suggested Ruzsa/Bose-Chowla as valid constructions. Both fail for large primes."

**Root cause:** The Architect read the state_of_affairs dead-end section but didn't apply it when
drafting the explore_1 brief. The dead-end text is a paragraph in the narrative; it may not have
been salient enough to block the brief from suggesting those constructions.

**Severity:** Moderate. explore_1 wasted multiple turns verifying that these formulas don't work.
This is known information and should not be re-investigated.

### [MODERATE] Research agent surveyed system state too slowly

**Observation:** research_1 spent the first portion of its session on theoretical analysis
(proving the average argument for Singer q=101 truncation, computing expected yields) — analysis
that was about to be rendered moot by exploit_1's breakthrough. The research report explicitly
notes: "I should have checked exploit_1 output immediately at session start. This would have
redirected research 15 minutes earlier."

**Evidence:** research_1 report item 2: "What information did I lack? — Current state of
exploit_1: I should have checked exploit_1 output immediately at session start."

**Root cause:** Research briefs don't instruct agents to check population outputs first.

**Severity:** Moderate. The web searches that would provide the most value for gen 3 (O'Bryant
tables, computational records for N=10000) were never completed because time ran out.

### [MINOR] SA runs consumed 228 seconds proving a predictable negative

**Observation:** exploit_2/sol01 (SA from 99-element seed, 114s) and exploit_2/sol03 (SA from
102-element seed, 114s) both failed to improve. Together they consumed 228 seconds (38% of
agent runtime) proving that SA doesn't work from these starting points.

**Evidence:** exploit_2 observations: "minimum blockers per non-member: 40 (candidate: element
9928)... Every non-member conflicts with ≥40 elements... making perturbation/SA approaches
essentially infeasible." The blocker count was established after sol02 (the q=101 breakthrough).
At that point, running sol03 SA should have been unnecessary.

**Root cause:** sol03 was planned before the blocker analysis revealed 40+ blockers. Once the
blocker analysis was done, the agent should have pivoted away from SA. The brief's structured
task list may have discouraged mid-session replanning.

**Severity:** Minor. SA results are now on record as negative evidence. The time cost was
acceptable given the generation's overall success.

## Missing Capabilities

### [CRITICAL] No web search for computational Sidon set records (two generations missed)

**Observation:** The single most important missing fact is: "What is the best known Sidon set
size for N=10000?" O'Bryant's 2004 arXiv survey (math/0407117) and related computational
databases may contain tables of best known values. Without this, agents cannot calibrate whether
103+ is achievable or whether 102 is already state-of-the-art.

**Evidence:** research_1 report: "Web searches for computational Sidon set records NOT completed
(interrupted)." evaluator report: "Published computational records for N=10000. Is 102 the known
best? This is the single most important piece of missing information." This was also requested
in gen 1 research (gen001 agent_gaps).

**Severity:** Critical. Two generations have passed without answering this. If 102 is the known
best, the strategy should focus on verification and publishing. If 105+ sets are published,
we need to understand their constructions immediately.

## Prompt Problems

### [MODERATE] Dead-end notes in State of Affairs not propagated to agent briefs

**Observation:** The state_of_affairs dead-end section contains: "Parabola/quadratic-residue
constructions: Mathematically incorrect for large primes." The Architect read this but explore_1's
brief still presented the carry-violating Ruzsa and Bose-Chowla formulas as valid options.

**Root cause analysis:** The Architect's prompt instructs it to read the state_of_affairs and
use it to inform briefs, but there's no structured field for "hard constraints to communicate
to each agent." Dead-end knowledge lives in prose form and may be overlooked when drafting briefs.

**Recommendation:** The Architect should include a "Dead Ends — Do Not Investigate" section in
every brief, explicitly listing constructions/approaches that are known to fail. This prevents
agents from rediscovering failures.

---

### [MINOR] Research briefs lack "check population outputs first" instruction

**Observation:** Research agents are instructed to analyze the system state, but not explicitly
instructed to check what other agents have already produced before beginning analysis. This
caused research_1 to spend time on Singer q=101 analysis when the population directory already
contained exploit_1's sol01.score with fitness=102.

**Recommendation:** Research agent briefs should include an explicit first step: "Before any
analysis, read `population/gen002/` to understand what has already been achieved this generation."

## Resource Issues

### [MINOR] Singer prime exhaustion creates a strategic inflection point

**Observation:** Singer constructions are now exhausted. All primes q from 97 to 109 have been
tested. q=101 is provably optimal (102 elements, all fitting in {0..10000}). The 40+ blocker
analysis confirms local search cannot improve from 102. This means all paths that worked in
gen 1 and gen 2 are now dead ends.

**Implication:** Gen 3 agents face a fundamentally harder problem. The 7-element gap from 102
to 109 cannot be closed by iterating on Singer. Resources should shift entirely to: (1)
literature search for published constructions, (2) correct Bose-Chowla, (3) Ruzsa's actual
construction, (4) large-k perturbation (k=10-20 removals), and (5) ILP.

**Risk:** Without new constructions, gen 3 agents may thrash on incremental Singer variants
that exploit_1 already proved futile (different polynomials, q=103/107/109 truncations).
Briefs must be explicit that Singer is exhausted.
